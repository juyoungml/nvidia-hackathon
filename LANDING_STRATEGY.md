# Landing page narrative

Primary promise: connect dispersed plant information so the engineer can see where to inspect and why.

1. **Outcome first:** actual DOE schematic, selected location, equipment role, related evidence and investigation value. Interactive source-navigation example. No fake sensor measurements attached to this diagram.
2. **Investigation burden:** sensor volume and fragmented sources require repeated searching and cross-checking. 50,000 is an illustrative scale, not our tested deployment.
3. **User workflow:** notice → locate → compare → decide. Each step explains what changes for the engineer.
4. **Architecture:** explain why Nano, Ultra and preparation have different jobs, after the reader understands the benefit.
5. **Prepared evidence:** actual source page shows text and diagram context. NVIDIA extraction/retrieval is a candidate implementation, not a completed integration.
6. **Verification:** actual PreDist fields, one Ultra run and its limits. Separate energy-equipment replay from power-station field validation.
7. **Economic hypothesis:** 8h baseline assumption → 30min target. Reduction in plant-clock identification time needs a controlled comparison; model-response seconds do not establish it.

Source boundaries: DOE generic schematic and PreDist district-heating incident are independent examples. See web/assets/SOURCES.md and data/README.md. The illustrative plant SVG remains available as an asset but is no longer the lead product visual.

Acceptance: a reader should be able to answer who uses this, what they receive, why it helps, what has actually run, and what is still a hypothesis—before caring about the model names.
